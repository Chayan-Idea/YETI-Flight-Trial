STANDARD OPERATING PROCEDURE

YETI FLIGHT TRIAL

STANDARD OPERATING PROCEDURE (SOP)

Document ID —Revision —Effective Date —Process Owner V&V / Safety / Testing / Assurance

1. PURPOSE & SCOPE

  • Define a controlled, traceable workflow for preparing, inspecting, operating and recovering a 500 kg-class logistics UAV YETI flight, including a nominal ~600 V DC battery system.
  • Applies to UAV propulsion, HV energy storage, GCS, ground equipment, personnel, flight data and incident response.
  • Use with the approved UAV manual, electrical safety procedures, test card, risk assessment, site emergency plan and applicable regulations.

2. ROLES & RESPONSIBILITIES

RoleResponsibility
Test Lead / Flight Test DirectorApprove trial readiness, briefing, go/no-go, stop-work decisions and records completeness.
PIC / Remote PilotConfirm operational readiness, remain within approved limits, command abort/recovery and report anomalies.
HV Authorised Person (HVAP)Control HV access, isolation, proving-dead and electrical work under the approved HV procedure; apply lockout/tagout (LOTO) where required.
Airframe / Electrical / Propulsion EngineerPerform assigned integrity checks; document defects; not to work on HV circuits unless authorized.
GCS / Test EngineerVerify recording, archive raw logs and correlate files using a unique Flight ID.
Safety / Assurance / Emergency LeadCoordinate hazard controls, emergency response, incident notification, investigation and return-to-service review.

3. NON-NEGOTIABLE SAFETY RULES

  • A ~600 V DC battery is potentially lethal and can sustain severe DC arc events. Treat all HV conductors as energized until isolated. Only trained and formally authorized personnel may access HV enclosures, operate HV isolation devices where designated, test HV circuits, or perform HV maintenance.
  • Use the specified PPE, insulated tools, barriers and approach boundaries from the approved electrical risk assessment.
  • Damaged, wet, overheated, swollen, leaking, smoking or impact-exposed battery packs must be treated as hazardous; do not touch, move, open, charge or reconnect them until assessed under the battery emergency plan by qualified personnel.
  • For smoke, hissing, venting, unusual heat, electrolyte leakage or suspected thermal runaway: raise alarm, evacuate/upwind to the site-defined safe distance, isolate the area and call the designated emergency response. Do not approach to retrieve data or equipment first.
  • Any failed or uncertain safety-critical check = NO-GO. PIC or Test Lead may stop the trial at any time. A crash, hard landing, electrical anomaly or safety-critical exceedance grounds the aircraft pending documented engineering and safety release.

4. TRIAL PREPARATION

  • Confirm approved test plan/card, objective, acceptance limits, aircraft ID, mass/CG and payload, configuration, software/firmware, battery-pack ID and propulsion setup.
  • Review current risk assessment, weather, site/airspace permissions, emergency access, fire response capability, communications, recovery zones, lost-link response and abort criteria.
  • Review aircraft maintenance status, open defects, prior anomalies, battery history/health, HV inspection status, insulation-monitoring status and calibration status.
  • Confirm GCS, telemetry, antennas, ground equipment, charger/power source, data storage, time synchronization, recording and backup location are ready.

5. PRE-FLIGHT CHECKS

5.1 Mechanical & Airframe Integrity

AreaCheckpoints — record PASS / FAIL / N/A and remarks
StructureFuselage, wings, tail, joints, hinges, fasteners, bonding, cracks, deformation, delamination, impact or transport damage.
ControlsLinkages, actuators/servos, attachment, free travel, direction, neutral, range, binding and interference per approved procedure.
Landing / launch / recoveryLanding gear, wheels/skids, launch/recovery fittings, restraints and recovery-system readiness.
PropulsionMotor/propeller condition, correct type/orientation, mounting, fasteners, clearance, wiring and signs of damage; propulsion disarmed during inspection.
Payload / CGPayload restraint, loading configuration, mass and centre of gravity within approved limits; doors/latches and access panels secured.
Final walk-aroundTools/covers removed, no loose objects, all required fasteners verified and aircraft configuration matches test card.

Follow the dedicated checklist as per iF standard.

5.2 Electrical & ~600 V DC Battery / HV Integrity

Check areaCheckpoint / acceptance evidence
Pack conditionCorrect approved pack and ID; enclosure secure; no impact marks, swelling, leakage, odour, abnormal heat, moisture ingress or damaged vents.
HV cabling/connectorsVisible cable insulation, routing, strain relief, connector locking, polarity keying, abrasion/chafing, contamination and enclosure integrity acceptable; no exposed conductors.
HV isolation & interlocksService disconnect, contactors, interlocks, pre-charge and emergency isolation devices visually/status checked per approved design procedure; no bypass or unexplained fault.
Protection / monitoringBMS status, cell-voltage spread, pack voltage/current, temperature sensors, insulation-monitoring and fault flags within aircraft/battery manufacturer limits.
LV / avionics supplyLV battery/DC-DC supply, fuses/protection, harnesses, grounding/bonding as designed, flight controller, GNSS, airspeed sensor, antennas and connectors healthy.
Battery readinessSOC/voltage, temperature, health and allowable charge/discharge limits meet the approved flight profile; pack securely restrained and thermal management unobstructed.
HV work controlNo exposed-HV work during routine pre-flight. If maintenance/testing is required, it must be a separate authorized HV task with isolation, LOTO, proving-dead and re-energization controls.
Electrical releaseNo active safety-critical faults, abnormal heating, insulation warning or unresolved defect; responsible engineer/HVAP sign-off recorded as required.

Follow the dedicated checklist as per iF standard.

5.3 GCS Daily Pre-Flight Checklist

AreaMinimum verification
GCS & powerConsole/computer, power/UPS, storage capacity, correct date/time and time synchronization.
Control & telemetryCorrect aircraft selected; command and telemetry links, antennas, link quality and alarms checked.
Mission & configurationApproved mission, geofence/limits, home point, map/coordinates, failsafe and return/land/abort actions verified.
Control checksApproved control-input and actuator/surface checks completed; correct direction and neutral positions confirmed.
HV/BMS/propulsion telemetryPack voltage/current/SOC, cell and pack temperatures, BMS, insulation monitor, ESC/motor telemetry and relevant warnings visible/recording as configured.
RecordingAP logs, telemetry, GCS screen recording, BMS/ESC logs and cameras enabled/tested; adequate storage and unique Flight ID assigned.
Emergency & go/no-goCrew communications, emergency contacts, recovery equipment, weather/site limits and checklist sign-off confirmed.

Follow the dedicated checklist as per iF standard.

5.4 Final Go / No-Go & Flight Execution

  • Brief all crew on test points, roles, weather, exclusion zones, communications, abort triggers, emergency landing/recovery and battery/HV hazards.
  • Confirm no personnel or equipment are in hazardous propulsion zones; clear the launch/recovery area and verify aircraft configuration against the approved test card.
  • Monitor flight-control health, link quality, battery voltage/current/temperature, BMS/insulation alerts, ESC/motor behaviour and aircraft response against approved limits.
  • Abort/recover if limits are exceeded, any safety-critical warning appears, control/telemetry degrades, environmental conditions deteriorate, or PIC/Test Lead judges the trial unsafe.

6. POST-FLIGHT SAFETY & INSPECTION

AreaPost-flight checkpoints
Make safeRecover and secure aircraft; disarm propulsion; inhibit unintended restart; establish exclusion around propeller/propulsion; follow approved shutdown sequence.
HV statusDo not open HV enclosures or disconnect loaded connectors. Isolate HV only as specified by the aircraft procedure and authorized role; observe required discharge/wait time and verify status before authorized work.
Battery conditionRecord pack ID, SOC/voltage, temperature, BMS alarms, cell data and any unusual heat, smell, noise, swelling, leakage or damage. Never handle a hot, venting, impact-damaged or electrically suspect battery merely to complete the checklist. After normal flight, place the battery in its approved storage/charging area and follow manufacturer-defined temperature, SOC, inspection and charging requirements.
Mechanical inspectionCheck airframe, control surfaces/linkages, motor/propeller, landing/recovery system, payload restraints, fasteners, wiring and connectors for damage, looseness, heat or abrasion.
Electrical/propulsion dataCapture AP, GCS, BMS and ESC logs before power-down where safe; record warnings, peak/abnormal values and shutdown events.
DispositionRecord defect, risk, owner and due date. An abnormal event or impact requires formal inspection and authorized review before the next flight.

7. CRASH / ABNORMAL EVENT — SAFE RECOVERY PLAN

Trigger this plan after a crash, hard landing, flyaway, loss of control/link, suspected battery/HV damage, smoke/fire, structural failure, injury/property damage or any event where aircraft energy state is uncertain. Life safety takes priority over aircraft or data recovery.

PhaseRequired actions
1 — Stop & alertPIC announces emergency and last known aircraft location; stop flight operations and launch activity. Notify Test Lead, site safety/emergency lead and emergency services as required.
2 — Secure people & siteAccount for personnel; keep bystanders away; establish and guard an exclusion zone based on the site emergency plan and incident hazards. Approach from a safe direction/upwind where appropriate; consider terrain, wind, smoke, traffic and secondary hazards.
3 — Assume energizedTreat the battery/HV bus, exposed wiring and damaged power electronics as energized. Do not touch aircraft, conductors, connectors, battery or conductive debris. Do not attempt improvised isolation or voltage checks.
4 — Fire / thermal eventFor smoke, flame, venting, hissing, unusual heat or suspected thermal runaway, withdraw/evacuate to the site-defined safe distance, raise alarm and let trained responders apply the approved battery-fire/incident plan. Do not approach to recover logs or extinguish without designated training/equipment.
5 — Scene & hazards assessmentOnly the designated incident lead and competent responders assess approach safety. Consider residual propulsion movement, stored electrical energy, damaged structures, sharp edges, fuel/chemical hazards if fitted, unstable payload, high-voltage lines and unsafe terrain.
6 — Stabilize & isolateOnly authorized responders/HV personnel may execute aircraft-specific emergency isolation after judging it safe. Follow the approved emergency isolation, LOTO, wait-time and verify-de-energized procedure. If isolation cannot be confirmed, maintain the exclusion zone and treat it as energized.
7 — Recovery authorizationNo personnel retrieve or move the wreckage until the incident lead confirms the area is safe and authorizes recovery. Use a documented recovery plan, suitable lifting/handling equipment and trained personnel; avoid loads on damaged battery enclosures or HV cables.
8 — Quarantine & transportSegregate and label damaged aircraft/battery. Do not charge, reconnect, open or transport a suspect battery in an ordinary vehicle. Specialist battery responders determine packaging, monitoring, movement and disposal under applicable rules.
9 — Evidence preservationAfter scene safety is confirmed, photograph the scene and aircraft before disturbance where possible; record location, orientation, damage, time and witnesses. Preserve AP/GCS/BMS/ESC logs, screen recordings, videos, photos, configuration and maintenance records. Do not power up or reset the system.
10 — Return to serviceGround the aircraft and affected battery/parts pending engineering inspection, incident investigation and written release by designated authorities. Review risk assessment and authorize corrective actions before any repeat trial.

This section is a framework, not a substitute for a site-specific emergency response plan, aircraft rescue manual, battery manufacturer instructions, HV switching procedure or local emergency-service direction. Define site-specific exclusion distances, emergency contacts, fire response, lifting points and responder roles before trials.

8. INCIDENT LOGBOOK & ROOT-CAUSE ANALYSIS (RCA)

  • Log promptly: Incident ID, date/time/site, Flight ID, aircraft and battery IDs, configuration, phase of flight, factual event sequence with timestamps, alarms, weather, injuries/damage, immediate actions, notifications and witnesses.
  • Preserve evidence only after the scene is safe. Record evidence source, collector and repository path.
  • RCA report: problem statement and impact; timeline; evidence and known facts; containment; causal analysis (5-Why, fault tree or equivalent); direct/contributing/systemic causes; corrective and preventive actions; owner/due date; effectiveness check and approval.
  • Separate verified facts from hypotheses; document unknowns. Track corrective actions to closure and formally approve return to service.

9. DAILY DATA UPLOAD & RECORDS

FolderRequired content
00_Admin_TestPlanApproved test plan/cards, risk assessment, permissions, configuration, briefing and go/no-go record with filled checklists.
01_AP_LogsAutopilot/flight-controller logs, telemetry and event logs.
02_GCS_ScreenRecordingFull GCS session recordings and file-to-Flight-ID index.
03_Battery_BMSBMS logs, pack ID, voltage/current, cell voltages, temperatures, alarms and health/cycle data.
04_ESC_PropulsionESC logs, motor/propulsion telemetry and test-equipment outputs.
05_Video_PhotoOnboard/ground videos and photographs; retain original quality and metadata.
06_Checklists_FlightReportsSigned mechanical/electrical/GCS checklists, flight summary, post-flight observations and action tracker.
07_Incidents_RCAIncident logbook, evidence index, investigation, RCA, corrective actions and closure evidence in case of major incidents.
  • Upload after every flight day to the approved repository; preserve raw originals and verify file readability, count and size. Record missing files and reasons.
  • Apply organization-approved access controls, backups, retention and revision control. Do not delete or overwrite incident evidence.

10. DOCUMENT CONTROL & RELEASE

  • Any deviation must be documented, risk-assessed and approved by the designated authority.
  • This SOP shall be reviewed and customized to align with the actual battery chemistry and pack design, aircraft emergency isolation architecture, manufacturer-specified limits, high-voltage (HV) safety requirements, PPE and approach boundaries, site-specific emergency contacts, exclusion zones, designated recovery and lifting points, and applicable records-retention requirements.
  • This SOP does not replace the aircraft flight manual, battery emergency instructions, approved test plan or applicable regulations.
